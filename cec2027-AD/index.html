01 / THE MOTIVATION

Aim

Autonomous driving requires effective optimisation and learning in dynamic environments with uncertain road-user behaviour, competing objectives, and costly evaluations. Systems must predict vehicle trajectories, learn driving policies, and balance safety, traffic efficiency, passenger comfort, and energy use. Evolutionary computation provides methods for searching model and policy spaces, exploring these trade-offs, and discovering challenging traffic scenarios. Learning can also support evolutionary search through predictive surrogates and adaptive search strategies. These connections motivate research in constrained optimisation, sample-efficient learning, and adaptation under uncertainty.

02 / RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

Scope

This special session brings together researchers and practitioners working on evolutionary optimisation and learning for autonomous driving. Its focus is the interaction between learning predictive models and driving policies, optimising system behaviour, and evaluating performance through scenario-based testing. We welcome neuroevolution, genetic programming, evolutionary reinforcement learning, multiobjective and quality-diversity optimisation, coevolution, and learning-assisted evolutionary search, including hybrid approaches using generative models and control. Contributions should make the role of evolutionary computation explicit and report relevant baselines, operating conditions, computational budgets, and evaluation measures. By connecting optimisation, learning, and testing, the session aims to identify methodological challenges and support reproducible assessment of the capabilities and limitations of autonomous driving systems.

  • 12Transfer, adaptation, and dynamic optimisation in evolutionary driving methods
  • 13Evolutionary optimisation of simulation parameters and domain randomisation
  • 14Evolutionary neural architecture search and model selection for driving applications
  • 15Evolutionary learning for cooperative driving and multi-agent coordination
  • 16Reproducible benchmarks and evaluation of evolutionary optimisation and learning

A shared discussion

Proposed activity

Subject to the conference programme and the number of accepted papers, we propose a short discussion on the interaction between evolutionary optimisation, learning, and testing for autonomous driving. The discussion would address sample efficiency, scenario coverage, computational cost, and the transferability of findings across driving conditions. Its format and duration will follow the programme allocation.
